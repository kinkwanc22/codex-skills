## 3.2 Original-Content Scope Control

Before rebuilding, divide the source body into major content units. A content unit is a real part of the article's answer, such as one reason, one promised rule, one step, one mechanism, one diagnostic distinction, or one stage in a how-to sequence. Do not count filler transitions or repeated examples as independent units.

Create an internal original-content control map containing:

- central conclusion;
- audience promise and relationship stage;
- original answer categories and their order, such as `为什么 -> 怎么做`, `信号 -> 判断`, or `错误 -> 机制 -> 动作`;
- topic-defining causal chain;
- body-promised count and item functions;
- distinctive source mechanisms or judgments that make this article recognizable;
- reusable short signature sentences, if any;
- source cases, dialogue, proof scenes, repetition, or weak supporting points that may be replaced.

Default scope:

- preserve 60%-80% of the source's viewpoint and mechanism functions;
- renew 20%-40% of viewpoint/supporting functions through new proof, supporting explanations, transitions, objection handling, and weak supporting points;
- wording may be fully reorganized for a natural 2.5 draft, so functional preservation does not require paragraph-level copying;
- normally preserve at least two topic-defining source mechanisms when the source contains them;
- preserve every top-level answer category. If the source promises both `为什么` and `怎么做`, the rebuilt source must still answer both rather than replacing them with a new list of general judgments;
- preserve the original count and item order when the order carries the argument. Change the count only when the user explicitly permits it or the body itself does not actually promise that number.

Operation replacement is measured separately from viewpoint preservation. Apply the 3.1 novelty logic inside every preserved viewpoint:

- identify the source's concrete operating units: exact actions, scripts, example dialogue, timing choices, step sequence, scene chain, tool, object, and proof feedback;
- replace the majority of those operating units, normally 60%-100%, while keeping the viewpoint, intended effect, relationship stage, and causal reason recognizable;
- a new operation must be functionally equivalent, not merely adjacent. If the viewpoint is `让她参与投入`, replace `买皮肤/洗衣服` with new time, planning, attention, choice, or action investments—not with an unrelated withdrawal tactic;
- replace old phrasing and surface details, but also replace the action logic enough that the new method can survive a side-by-side duplication check;
- one distinctive source method may remain only when it is inseparable from the public mother topic; record the exception and extend it with a different scene, timing, consequence, and proof;
- do not preserve an old operation by changing one noun, swapping occupations, or moving the same dialogue to another location;
- keep the body-promised count stable unless the user permits a count change. New operations must fully occupy the original count slots rather than adding a parallel list.

Cases are the preferred replacement zone. A long copied case may be replaced by several compact theory-first student cases, but each new case must prove the same preserved mechanism or step. Do not replace the source mechanism merely because the old case is unusable.

The monetization structure is an overlay, not a replacement skeleton. Apply it to the preserved answer path as follows:

- derive the hook from the source's central conclusion;
- use a precise pain scene to enter the source's first real argument;
- keep the source's main explanation and solution order;
- add realistic consequences and new evidence where the source needs proof;
- place compact student cases immediately after the preserved theories or methods they demonstrate;
- add objection handling and person-specific variables only after the source's public answer is complete;
- end with one topic-specific fan-group CTA.

Do not force every monetization stage into a new standalone section. If the source already contains the relevant pain, consequence, mechanism, or solution, strengthen that passage instead of creating a parallel structure.

Exceeding the 40% functional replacement ceiling requires either explicit user authorization or a recorded reason that the affected source units are duplicated, unusable, contradictory, or absent. A desire to make the article feel newer is not sufficient reason.

## 3.2 Theory-First Student-Case Modification

Student cases are designed before expansion and written into the rebuilt source itself. They are proof of a theory that has just been explained, not opening hooks, free-floating scenes, or instructions sent separately to Gemini.

The mandatory local order for every case unit is:

1. Gary first completes the current theory, mechanism, judgment, or method in plain language;
2. transition naturally with wording such as `我之前有个学员就卡在这里` or `我给你们讲一个学员身上的典型情况`;
3. state the student's original problem, wrong action, or decision point;
4. state the specific adjustment Gary gave, or the action the student changed;
5. show observable feedback, changed participation, changed evaluation, or changed relationship position;
6. immediately return to the just-explained theory and state what the case proves.

The case may not appear before the theory it proves. Do not open a numbered point with a student story and explain the theory afterward. Do not scatter anonymous generic scenes between theoretical sentences. One theory block may use one compact student case; a closely related group of points may share one case only when the mapping remains obvious.

Default density:

- every 3.2 article uses two or three compact student cases by default, regardless of total length or list count;
- numbered/list source: select only the two or three highest-leverage theories or methods for student-case proof; the remaining items continue through direct mechanism, scene, contrast, or consequence analysis;
- non-list mechanism source: place two or three student cases after distinct and important causal turns;
- do not mechanically force a case after every point, and do not exceed three cases unless the user explicitly requests a denser case version.

Every student case must contain all four proof fields:

1. `student_problem`: the student's topic-specific original action, sentence, timing choice, or relationship scene;
2. `gary_adjustment`: the concrete change Gary asked the student to make;
3. `observable_feedback`: the other person's observable response or the student's changed judgment/position;
4. `theory_return`: an immediate return to the theory just explained, including the reality cost or next-action standard.

Cases must be compact. Normally one to three short paragraphs are enough. Do not let a case acquire a long biography, consultation history, repeated coaching steps, or miracle ending.

Forbidden case patterns:

- one man fails, contacts Gary, follows instructions, the woman becomes active, and the relationship flips;
- one protagonist or one consultation event occupying most of the article;
- cases copied or lightly paraphrased from the supplied source;
- the same party, coffee, late-night chat, refusal, and reversal sequence reused across unrelated titles;
- generic cases that could move under another title after changing only the pronouns;
- a case appearing before the theory it is supposed to prove;
- a student label with no Gary adjustment, observable feedback, or theory return;
- fabricated student/client cases presented as verified outcomes.

If the user supplies a real student record, anonymize private details and set `case_source_status` to `supplied_student_record`. Otherwise retrieve from `02_B高价值知识/08_提炼记录/外部资料_Gary匿名复合案例.jsonl` before composing a case. A retrieved `GCOMP-*` entry is only a candidate with status `external_case_candidate_requires_adaptation`: open the card, keep one continuous source scene, and extract the student problem, Gary adjustment, observable feedback, and theory return before writing. Once adopted into a run, record that run as `adapted_anonymized_composite`. Do not stitch the card's dispersed automatic clues into a case, and do not invent a name, exact date, exact client volume, screenshots, guaranteed reversal, or other checkable claims. Only when no retrieved candidate fits may a new internally labelled `anonymized_composite_student_case` be assembled from traceable atomic knowledge. In the oral manuscript, introduce either kind as a typical situation rather than claiming a verified individual result.

If the user asks for one complete case through the whole article, stop using 3.2 and route to `2.6 Single Case`.

## 3.2 Monetization Architecture Override

3.2 inherits the complete 3.1 pre-transplant architecture, but replaces 3.1's ordinary conversion placement and dual-CTA expectation with this single-CTA monetization architecture. This is the only conversion override; all other inherited 3.1 rules remain active.

Use this hidden sequence as an overlay on the controlled original answer path:

1. a strong-conclusion traffic hook that preserves broad stopping power;
2. precise audience and relationship-scene identification;
3. the concrete wrong action the viewer is likely taking;
4. the topic-native dark mechanism or reality behind the problem;
5. the observable cost of continuing the wrong action;
6. substantial numbered judgments, distinctions, or methods that deliver the public answer;
7. evidence and objection handling, followed by the person-specific variable gap;
8. one final fan-group CTA.

Do not expose labels such as `圈人群`, `痛点`, `放大后果`, `展示方案`, `破顾虑`, `营销`, `CTA`, or `成交结构` in the manuscript. The sequence must read as one continuous Gary argument rather than a sales template.

The traffic hook must come from the source's central conclusion, conflict, or result. Do not invent a broader or adjacent conclusion merely because it sounds stronger. Move into the precise viewer scene immediately after the hook so traffic and conversion work in sequence instead of competing.

The article must deliver a useful general answer before conversion. Do not withhold the promised list, mechanism, distinction, or method merely to force group entry. Cases, contrasts, and dialogue slices belong inside the explanatory and method sections as evidence; they must not become testimonials pasted after the advice.

Evidence and objection handling may use:

- compact typical scenes;
- observable behavior and response changes;
- positive/negative scene contrasts;
- supplied real records when their status is known;
- two or more topic-specific variables showing why the same surface signal can require a different judgment.

Do not fabricate client/student outcomes, screenshot claims, transaction results, or guaranteed reversals. A general article may explain the shared rule completely while reserving one individualized stage judgment or next-action choice that genuinely depends on the viewer's own evidence.

There is only one explicit conversion action in the whole article: enter the Douyin profile fan group near the end. Do not place an explicit group invitation in the middle. Do not simultaneously ask for likes, follows, comments, direct messages, a course purchase, or another destination.

Treat the final conversion paragraph and the fixed Gary sign-off as one ending conversion unit. The paragraph immediately before the fixed sign-off must specify:

- one topic-specific evidence package the viewer should bring;
- one bounded judgment or next-action decision the group helps clarify;
- no promise of a full private consultation, guaranteed result, or unlimited service.

If the source is correct but Gemini dropped or distorted it, rerun the same complete 2.5 prompt plus frozen source through `--isolated`. Do not fix the problem by attaching a case supplement.
